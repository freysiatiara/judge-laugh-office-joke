# Joke candidates — head-to-head against the judge's 5 criteria

Judge criteria, in order: (1) about office life and lands as a joke, (2) true to
office life, (3) surprising turn / fresh angle / unexpected last word, NOT a pun
or stock line, (4) tight, one line, reads cleanly aloud, (5) original.

## Candidates

1. "The printer jammed on my resignation letter, so technically I'm still employed by a paper jam." (94 chars)
2. "I told my boss I work best under pressure, so now every deadline is yesterday." (78 chars)
3. "The coffee machine broke, so for two hours the office rediscovered conversation, then panic." (92 chars)
4. "I asked for a raise and got a pizza party, which is how I learned my salary is paid in pepperoni." (97 chars)
5. "I put 'per my last email' in my out-of-office reply, so now even my absence sounds passive-aggressive." (102 chars)
6. "I wrote 'as discussed' in an email about something we never discussed, and everyone nodded along." (97 chars)
7. "I asked HR if 'unlimited PTO' means I can take Friday off; they said unlimited, not available." (94 chars)

## Head-to-head

- #1 vs #4: both true and tight. #4's pizza-party-instead-of-a-raise premise is a
  well-worn meme; #1's "still employed by a paper jam" is a fresher, more absurd
  reframe with a genuinely unexpected last word. #1 wins on (3) and (5).
- #1 vs #3: #3 has a nice two-beat arc (conversation, then panic), but its kicker
  is a soft observation; #1's kicker reframes the whole setup. #1 wins on (3).
- #1 vs #7: #7's "unlimited, not available" is sharp, but PTO jokes are common
  and the turn is a wordplay-adjacent contrast; #1's turn is a fresh angle, not
  wordplay. #1 wins on (3) and (5).
- #1 vs #5: #5's mechanism is clever but needs a beat of parsing and lands as wit
  more than a laugh; #1 reads cleanly aloud in one breath. #1 wins on (1) and (4).
- #1 vs #2: #2 is clean, but "deadline is yesterday" is a known formulation and
  the line is the shortest (78 chars, below target). #1 wins on (3) and (5).
- #1 vs #6: #6's premise is true and original, but "everyone nodded along" is a
  gentle observation with the weakest laugh of the set. #1 wins on (1).

## Pick

Candidate #1 — 94 characters, 94 bytes, two beats (printer jams on the
resignation letter -> the jam itself keeps the narrator employed). The joke
lives only in main.py as the JOKE constant.
